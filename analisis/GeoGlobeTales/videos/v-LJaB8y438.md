Why did the U.S. plant a massive wall of trees from Canada to Texas? 🌳 East of the Rocky Mountains lies the Great Plains, a flat, dry, wind-swept region long considered unsuitable for farming. But with the rise of tractors and deep plowing in the early 20th century, agriculture expanded rapidly. Prairie grasses with deep roots were destroyed, leaving the soil exposed 🌾 During the 1930s drought, constant winds lifted loose soil into the air, creating giant dust storms known as Black Blizzards 🌪 These storms darkened the skies, caused deadly dust pneumonia and forced millions to flee their homes, pushing the U.S. toward an environmental disaster. To stop the crisis, the Great Plains Shelterbelt Project was launched 🌲 A 100-mile-wide strip of over 220 million trees was planted to act as windbreaks, reducing wind speed by up to 50%. The result: less erosion, more moisture, and the recovery of farmland.
